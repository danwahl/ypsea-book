# Deep Research Prompt - Chapter 4: Probably

Please research the strongest arguments against veganism and the genuine uncertainties in animal ethics. I want to steel-man opposition views and honestly examine edge cases, while ultimately showing why uncertainty supports rather than undermines the case for plant-based diets.

## Key Research Areas:

### 1. Consciousness Skepticism
- Best arguments against animal consciousness (philosophical zombies)
- Higher-order thought theories and their implications
- The problem of other minds applied to animals
- Discontinuity theories of consciousness
- What would need to be true for animals not to suffer

### 2. Population Ethics and Existence
- The replaceability argument for farm animals
- Total vs. average utilitarianism debates
- Lives worth living threshold discussions
- Non-identity problem applied to farm animals
- Would farm animals prefer to exist with suffering vs. not exist?

### 3. Wild Animal Suffering
- Scale of suffering in nature vs. agriculture
- Habitat displacement arguments
- Whether farming prevents worse natural deaths
- Intervention vs. non-intervention ethics
- Recent work on wild animal welfare

### 4. Cultural and Subsistence Arguments
- Indigenous hunting and fishing practices
- Pastoral societies and animal husbandry
- Food sovereignty and colonialism concerns
- Arctic and other environments unsuitable for plant agriculture
- Distinguishing subsistence from industrial practices

### 5. Biological/Nutritional Edge Cases
- Evidence for nutrients only/best found in animal products
- Genetic variations affecting plant-based diet viability
- Child development and pregnancy concerns
- Elite athlete performance debates
- Bioavailability and absorption issues

### 6. Consciousness Spectrum Problems
- Bivalves (oysters, mussels) and ganglia vs. brains
- Insect sentience evidence and uncertainties
- Honey and beekeeping ethics
- Where/how to draw lines
- Microorganism consideration boundaries

### 7. Future Scenarios
- Cultivated meat timelines and limitations
- "Humane" animal agriculture possibilities
- Closed-loop systems and regenerative claims
- High-welfare farming economic viability

Please present the strongest version of each argument before addressing why it might fail. Include recent philosophical work on moral uncertainty and how to act under it.
